# M2: the campaign

M2 makes the gala one job in a season of thirteen. There are four venues with three jobs each, and Mae's house party is the finale. The other pieces are:

- **Mae's back room:** the board of jobs, her briefings, the take, and buying a new face.
- **The Circuit:** seeded remixes of the jobs, and a weekly contract.
- **The phone:** spoofed caller ID.
- **Text mode:** suggested lines.
- **Comfort settings.**
- **A job report you can share.**

The people of Caldera remember the crew from job to job. This page is the contract for building a venue and its jobs. It also lists what the rest of M2 adds.

## Files

Each venue has a folder, `game/venues/<venue>/`, holding:

- `<venue>.gd`: the building, `class_name <Venue> extends Venue` (`Marlin`, `Tideline`, `VantageTower`, `MaeHouse`).
- `<venue>_cast.gd`: the people, `class_name <Venue>Cast` (`MarlinCast`).
- `<job>_job.gd` for each job: `class_name <Venue><Job>Job extends JobDef` (`MarlinAboardJob`, `TidelineFavorJob`, `MaeHousePartyJob`). The path is the one in `Jobs.PATHS`.

Alongside the folder:

- **Tests:** `tests/<venue>_tests.gd`. The runner finds it, and `--only=<venue>` runs it alone.
- **Props:** `tools/blender/<venue>_props.py` writes `assets/models/<venue>/`. Prefix model names with the venue (`marlin_bar`, `tideline_slot`), because `Kit` finds every model under `assets/models` by name and names must be unique.
- **Textures:** if a venue needs its own, they go in `assets/textures/<venue>/`, from `tools/<venue>_textures.py`.
- **MegaKit pieces:** pieces of the Downtown City MegaKit the Meridian doesn't use go in `tools/megakit/<venue>.txt`, one name a line. Then run `python3 tools/vendor_megakit.py "<quaternius-assets>/Downtown City MegaKit[Source]/Exports/glTF (Godot)"`.
- **Screenshots:** `docs/screenshots/<venue>/`, with its README, and `docs/screenshots/jobs/<job>.png`.

A venue's work stays in these files. The engine's files are shared, so don't change them for one venue:

- `game/job/`, `game/brain/`, `game/people/`, `game/world/venue.gd`, `game/world/kit.gd`, `game/ui/`, `game/net/`
- `tools/screenshot.gd`, `tools/blender/props.py`
- `tests/venue_checks.gd`, `tests/job_harness.gd`

When the engine really can't do something, make the smallest additive change you can. That means a new method, a new signal, or a new `JobDef` field whose default changes nothing for other jobs. Put it in a commit of its own whose subject starts `Engine:`, and say what it is and why.

## The venue

A venue extends [`Venue`](../game/world/venue.gd). The Meridian ([`meridian.gd`](../game/venues/meridian/meridian.gd)) is the one to copy.

**`_init()`** sets three things:

- `zones`: room name to AABB.
- `zone_order`: the order `zone_at` checks them in, smaller rooms inside bigger ones first.
- `room_layers`: each zone's render layer, 2 to 20.

**`_build()`** runs in this order:

1. `_make_rooms([...])`.
2. Each room, built with `_in(zone)` and the building blocks: `_floor`, `_ceiling`, `_wall` (with doorways and windows as holes), `_m` and `_box` for models, `_solid` for collision, `_omni` and `_spot` for lights, and `_probe`.
3. `_make_doors(DOOR_SPECS)`, then the graph, the views, `_apply_layers()` and `_dress(self)`.

A venue's own materials go in `_more_mat()`, and its own wall styles in `_more_span()` and `_more_wall_top()`.

### What it has to provide

- **`doors`**: `HotelDoor`s by name, made by `_make_doors()`.
  - Spec keys: `at`, `w`, `h`, `to`, `model`, and optionally `double`, `auto` and `thick`.
  - Door names are what jobs lock, open and talk about, so name them plainly: `gangway`, `safe_room`, `count_room`.
- **`uses`**: things to use, by id, made by `_use(id, at, size, model)`.
  - An item lying somewhere is a use whose id is where it lies (`"desk"`) and whose meta `use` is `"item:<item id>"`.
  - Keypads are uses too, and so is anything the job decides the meaning of: `"lift_call"`, `"wardrobe"`, `"case"`.
- **`spots`**: where people stand, each a `Transform3D` facing its +Z.
  - Every cast member's spot, and every spot in a routine.
  - `crew_0` and `crew_1` (where the crew starts).
  - The office's `held` and guard spots.
  - The spot that counts as inside, if there is one.
  - Every spot is also a point.
- **`points` and `links`**: where people walk. A link is `[a, b, door]`.
  - Characters follow the line between two points, up and down included, so stairs and gangways are links from bottom to top.
  - A link straight up (same x and z) is a lift: a character rides it in one step.
- **`extras`**: the crowd, by zone, as standing `Transform3D`s. The job says what they wear.
- **`views` and `view_about`**: camera views set with `_set_views({name: [from, at]})`, and a line saying what each shows. Have at least six, covering every room and every job's key moment.
- **`clocks`**, if there are wall clocks, and **`environment()`** for the world.

### Rules for the building

- **Axes.** X runs east and Z south, and the main floor is y = 0. Other decks and floors sit wherever they like. Storeys of 3–5 m are fine, and zones say which floor is which.
- **Stairs** are a sloped collision box (30° at most) under stepped meshes, with points at the foot and the head.
- **Lifts.** The crew ride with `job.move_crew(crew_id, spot)`, called from the lift's use. Their device fades and puts them there.
- **Lights.** Each room lights only itself. A room's meshes go on its layer, and its lights cull to that layer and layer 1. No more than four shadowed lights in the whole venue.
- **Collision.** Walls get collision from `_wall`, models from `_box` or `_solid`. People and the crew must be able to walk every link, and nothing may stand in a spot, a link or the crowd. `VenueChecks` checks all of this.
- **Budget.** Keep it to 60 fps on a Steam Deck: modest triangle counts, few lights, and the kit's 1K textures. The Meridian is the bar.
- **Props.** Write a `tools/blender/<venue>_props.py` that does `sys.path.insert(0, os.path.dirname(__file__)); import props`.
  - Make each prop with `props.Prop(name)` and the shape helpers (`box`, `cyl`, `lathe`, `tube`, `prism`...).
  - Add materials with `props.MATERIALS.update({...})`.
  - Write each prop with `props.export(prop, path)` to `assets/models/<venue>/<name>.glb`.
  - Follow `props.py`'s conventions: Godot's axes, metres, floor props on y = 0 facing +Z, and material names that Godot dresses (`velvet_*`, `glass*`, `bulb`).
  - Run it with `/tmp/claude-0/bpy/venv/bin/python` (Blender as a Python module).
  - Moving parts are their own named nodes, so `job.show_node()` can hide them (Jacket_1..5).

## The cast

The cast lives in `<Venue>Cast`:

- `static func all() -> Array[CharacterSheet]`, `static func sheet(id)`.
- One static function per character, building a [`CharacterSheet`](../game/brain/character_sheet.gd) as `MeridianCast` does.
- Shared facts as constants, like `SITUATION`.

There are 8–15 characters per venue. The sheet is the character, so fill all of it:

- **Who they are:** `name`, `job` and `post`.
- **Their role and kind:** `role`, and whether they are `halberd`, `staff` or carry a `radio`.
- **Their body and voice:** `look`, `person_job`, `voice` and `voice_speed`.
- **How they think:** `manner`, `routine`, `knows`, `never`, `checks`, `weaknesses`.
- **What they can do:** `actions` and `checks_with` (action to colleague).
- **Secrets:** `secrets`, the trust-locked facts that are never in the prompt.
- **Where they stand:** `spot` and `door` (the door they keep).
- **Fallback lines:** `hesitations`, `fallbacks`, `not_heard` and `phone_hello`.
- **Scripted replies,** for no model: `replies`, as `{"words", "say", "tone", "do", "believes", "up"}`, first match wins.

**Voices** are Kokoro's:

- American women: `af_heart`, `af_alloy`, `af_aoede`, `af_bella`, `af_jessica`, `af_kore`, `af_nicole`, `af_nova`, `af_river`, `af_sarah`, `af_sky`.
- American men: `am_adam`, `am_echo`, `am_eric`, `am_fenrir`, `am_liam`, `am_michael`, `am_onyx`, `am_puck`.
- British women: `bf_alice`, `bf_isabella`, `bf_lily`.
- British men: `bm_daniel`, `bm_fable`, `bm_george`, `bm_lewis`.

`bf_emma` is Mae's (`GameConfig.MAE_VOICE`) and nobody else's. Give everyone in a venue a voice of their own where you can.

**Character ids are people.** An id is the same person wherever they turn up, and memories follow the id.

- A venue's three jobs share its cast, each with that night's posts and knowledge.
- Halberd's people move between venues. When someone from an earlier venue turns up, use their id and their sheet (`MeridianCast.teo()`, then change the post, spot and what they know), and list that venue in the job's `memory_from`.
- New ids must not clash with anyone else's: use the character's first name, and if two venues pick the same name, rename one.

**Halberd** guards the four venues. A Halberd guard has `halberd = true`, wears `guard` (or a look of the venue's own on the halberd suit), and is the only kind who holds the crew.

## A job

A job extends [`JobDef`](../game/job/job_def.gd), sets its data in `_init()`, and overrides the hooks it needs. [`gala_job.gd`](../game/venues/meridian/gala_job.gd) is the worked example. `JobDef`'s own comments document every field.

### The data

- **Who and where.** `id`, `venue_id`, `number`, `title` and `venue_name`.
- **The words.** `briefing` (Auntie Mae, in her own voice: warm, sharp, "love"; `briefer` and `briefer_voice` when someone else gives it), `goal`, `situation`, `prize`, `take` (the table above).
- **The clock.** `start` and `end` in minutes after midnight, `end_text`, and `seconds_per_minute`. A night is 20–40 real minutes: 40 game minutes at the gala's 16 s a minute is about 11 real minutes of clock, and talking stretches it.
- **Covers.** `covers` and `cover_order` set the job's covers (two to four).
  - Each cover has `title`, `names` (a man's and a woman's), `job`, `story`, `outfit`, `for` (who it's made for) and `lines`. The lines are at least three suggested lines for text mode, with `%s` for the cover's name.
  - `outfits`, `staff_looking`, `staff_rooms`, `looks` and `extras` dress the crew and the crowd. Looks are shaped like `Person.JOBS`: the painted outfits, or the `halberd_suit` and `evening_dress` wardrobe with the job's colours. Don't make new wardrobe outfits in M2.
- **The night's script.** `routine`, `chatter` and `events`, with times.
- **Doors and keypads.** `locked`, `unlocked`, `open`, `lockdown_doors`, `lockdown_text`, `door_texts`, `keypads` (four-digit codes) and `office`.
- **Things.** `items` (papers, prizes, keys, a clipboard) and `actions`, the job's own actions with their `kind`.
- **What actions do.** `effects`: open, flag, outfit, walk and toast.
- **The phone.** `phone` (numbers to ring) and `caller_ids`.
- **Mae's calls.** `mae_calls`, the calls Mae makes when you play alone.
- **How it ends.** `outcomes` (every way it can end, with a title and a line) and `wins`.
- **Inside.** `inside_zone` and `inside_text`, for jobs that are about getting somewhere.
- **Memories.** `memory_from`, the venues whose people this cast shares.

### The hooks

| Hook | What it's for |
|---|---|
| `venue()` | The building, unbuilt |
| `cast()` | The sheets |
| `give_items()` | What the crew carries in |
| `setup(job)` | Anything the data can't set up |
| `use()` | The venue's uses: a door's own rules, a wardrobe, the safe, the case, the lift (`job.move_crew`) |
| `prompt_for()` | What Interact says |
| `can_take()` | Whether an item can be taken |
| `on_action()` | An action that does more than `effects` can |
| `lookup()` | What a colleague answers a check with: `Job.fact(verdict, text, answer)` from game facts and what the crew told the asker. This is where lies hold up or don't |
| `on_routine()`, `on_event()`, `tick()` | The night going on |
| `check_end()`, `at_end()` | How it ends |
| `on_heat()`, `on_seen()`, `allowed_back()` | How the venue reacts |
| `on_mae()` | What Mae's calls do |
| `report()` | The job's own lines on the report |

### What a job can do with the `Job` it's handed

| Method | What it does |
|---|---|
| `set_goal(crew_id or 0, text)` | Changes the goal on the HUD |
| `give_item`, `place_item`, `holder`, `take` | Moves items between people and places |
| `show_node(name, visible)` | Shows or hides part of the venue on every device |
| `open_door`, `open_for`, `set_door`, `lock`, `shut_for_night` | Doors |
| `witness(c, text, by, heat)` | Staff who saw something take note |
| `tell(char_id, crew_id, text)` | Notes something said to a character |
| `move_crew(crew_id, spot)` | Takes someone somewhere at once |
| `finish(outcome)` | Ends the night |
| `flags` | The night's own flags |
| `crew_by_id(id).flags` | A crew member's flags |

### At least three ways through

Every job has at least three ways to the win. Each has a different person to work and a different lie to tell, so the job plays differently with each cover. For example:

- Talk past the person on the door.
- Get hired by the person who needs staff.
- Have Mae (or a crewmate) ring someone so a check comes back good.

Every way works alone, with Mae's calls standing in for a second crew member, and with two on LAN. A way may use a tool (a forged paper, the phone, a keypad code someone gives up) but never needs one that isn't in the job.

Write each way's facts into the sheets (who knows what, who checks with whom), its rules into the hooks (`lookup`, `on_action`, `use`), and its test into `tests/<venue>_tests.gd`.

### Words

- **Plain and short,** in the game's words: the crew, the job, the take, Halberd, the Rose, and Auntie Mae (Mae for short).
- **American spelling** in everything a player reads or hears: favor, color, center.
- **People speak like people.** No narration in lines, and nothing the character couldn't know. Mae's briefings are a paragraph in her voice. The goal is one line.

## Tests

`tests/<venue>_tests.gd` is a set of tests (see `tests/run_tests.gd`). Use the shared pieces:

- **[`VenueChecks`](../tests/venue_checks.gd):**
  - `built(t, venue, open_doors)`
  - `structure(t, venue, label, {spot: zone})`
  - `standing_room`
  - `walkable` (build it with the doors open)
  - `doors_block`
  - `job_data(t, def)` for each job
  - `routines(t, def)` for each job
- **[`JobHarness`](../tests/job_harness.gd):** plays a job with no scene and no model.
  - `start(t, def, covers)`
  - `face`, `stand`, `stand_at`, `say`, `say_to` (a line and its answer), `answered`, `last_do`, `walk`, `clock_to`, `done`

A test for each way through each job plays it with scripted brains: talk, show papers, ring someone, use things, and check the job ends the way it should (`job.done` and the report's outcome). Tests also cover:

- The ways it goes wrong: a lie that doesn't check out, Lockdown, the office.
- The job's own rules.
- The scripted replies: each character's key words give the reply their way needs.

Run the script check and the tests before every commit:

```sh
godot --headless --path . --import    # a new class_name needs this before the script check finds it
godot --headless --path . tools/check_scripts.tscn
godot --headless --path . tests/run_tests.tscn -- --only=<venue>
```

Then run the whole suite once at the end.

## Screenshots

`tools/screenshot.tscn` shoots any venue whose jobs exist:

```sh
VK_ICD_FILENAMES=/usr/share/vulkan/icd.d/lvp_icd.json xvfb-run -a -s "-screen 0 1280x800x24" \
  godot --path . --resolution 1280x800 tools/screenshot.tscn -- --all=docs/screenshots group=<venue>
VK_ICD_FILENAMES=... xvfb-run ... tools/screenshot.tscn -- --all=docs/screenshots group=jobs --only=<job>,<job>,<job>
```

- The first command writes every view of the venue, with its first job's cast, and the README.
- The second writes each job's first moment, from where the crew starts. Shooting with `--only` leaves the README alone.
- Look at the pictures. What a player would see wrong is the bar: floating props, gaps in walls, dark rooms, people in walls, and lights leaking between rooms.

## The rest of M2

- **Mae's back room** (the title):
  - The campaign board: each venue's jobs, open in order (`Progress.is_open`), with what came off and how (Clean, Quiet, Silver).
  - Mae's briefing, spoken in her voice, with the covers.
  - The season's take.
  - A new face when Halberd has yours (`Progress.burned`, `Progress.buy_face()`).
- **Memory.** `Progress` keeps what each character remembers of the crew (`Job.report().memories`, `Progress.memories_of()`), and Halberd's file (a face held at any job is burned until a new one is bought).
- **The phone.** Ring any number on the job's list as anyone (`caller_ids`). Alone, ask Mae to make her calls.
- **Text mode** offers the cover's suggested lines.
- **Comfort:**
  - A third-person camera that follows calmly.
  - Field of view.
  - Subtitles that say who and where ("Teo, behind you").
  - A mind-your-language filter on what's shown and spoken.
- **The report.** Save it as a picture, or copy its text.
- **The Circuit.** After the campaign (or with Open every job), a night's number remixes a job (`Circuit`):
  - The number picks the job, and one or two rules from `Circuit.RULES`: the clock runs fast, Halberd's on edge, jumpy staff, a friendly crowd, Halberd has your faces, clean or nothing, talk only.
  - People's rounds move up to ten minutes either way, each character's all together so they keep their order, and their chatter with them. Anyone with a round near one of the job's `events` or the end stays put, since the job may count on them.
  - The take scales with the rules, in steps of $500.
  - `JobDef.circuit` keeps what was done. `JobDef.sheets()`, `Job.setup()` and `Job.report()` call `Circuit.on_sheets`, `on_setup` and `on_report`, which do nothing on the season's own jobs.
  - The same number is the same night on every device: the host sends it with the job (`Session.circuit`, PROTOCOL 3).
  - Each week's contract is the week's number (202642 for week 42 of 2026, counted as releases count weeks), the same for everyone. Its board is M3's, online; `Progress.circuit` keeps each night's best on this device.
  - `--circuit=<number>` plays a night straight from the command line.
